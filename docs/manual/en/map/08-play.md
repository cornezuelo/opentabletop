# Playing a trip

**Play**, on the switch at the top (<kbd>P</kbd>), moves your party around the map. The bar on the left then holds only what play uses: **Select**, **The trip**, the World and the Oracle, and the keys of the tools that edit the map do nothing, so a slip can't paint over it; **Edit** (<kbd>P</kbd> again) comes back to the tool you were using. Play always opens on **The trip**, whatever tool or panel was open (a cell is still a click away with **Select**); with no trip yet, **New trip** is the button that stands out. A trip plays in one of two modes (**Play mode**, whose help sums them up). Going to Play selects the party token (the Tokens tool, in Edit, keeps it selected, and there you change its icon, color and halo).

## Simple mode

Just the party token and its trail: click a cell to place the party, click another and it jumps straight there: no route, no travel time, nothing happens on the way. No rolls. To walk cell by cell with time, terrain and checks, use **With rules**. **Show trail** draws where it has been, curving like the map's roads and trails (**Straight lines** draws it, and the planned route, from cell centre to cell centre). Both run a little to one side of the cells' centres, the trail on the left of its way and the route on the right, so they never sit on a road or river they follow, nor on each other; **Clear trail** and **Remove party** reset it.

## With rules

The Travel Engine runs the trip and the Oracle rolls the checks. The [Travel app](../travel/01-getting-started.md) plays the same systems without a map, and the [Packs app](../packs/01-getting-started.md) edits them.

1. Check the **system**: its campaign's (shared by the maps and trips in it), shown here and chosen in **Map settings → Campaign → System**: the Generic rules (Core's, when the campaign chooses none), or another pack's system (e.g. the Grey Marches). A system without travel rules plays no trips: Play says so, and its world, oracle and factions still play. It brings the travel rules, the tables they roll, the calendar and the weather. On one line, the system's name, the season to start in (or, with the world clock running, its date: no season to choose) and **New trip**, which restarts time, the party's sheet and journal, keeping the party where it is. With no party on the map yet, it says so: the trip starts on the cell you click.
2. Click a cell to place the party, then click the **destination**: the route is drawn.
3. The buttons are the system's actions, such as **March**, **Camp** or **Rest** (each system declares which actions it has, and when they can be taken). Its march goes along the route as far as you choose beside it (to the destination, one cell…), until the system's rule for marching stops the party (at night, when the day's hours are spent…, as the system says) or a check needs you; then **Go on** goes on with it. **Wait to go on** lets time pass where the party is until it can march again, living it, with the system's halt (camp…) if it can be taken: for a day nothing else can be done (lost, no food to camp). The [World clock](12-world.md#with-a-trip-going-on) moves the trip on too: with a route planned, **Next day** travels along it.

A strip over the map has two rows. Above, where the trip stands: the day, time and season, where the party is, the weather in the system's words (as the table that rolled it said it, _Grey and mild_), the marching hours used and the time spent on the way to the next cell, when one takes longer than a march. Below, what happened in your last step, story first: its last three story lines (a toll, an omen, a blow), the newest brightest; with none, the journal's newest line of the rules (a check rolled, a piece laid); hover one for all of it when it's long. Then where the party is heading. When the trip is paused (a check waiting for you, a step that stops for you, a foe's turn waiting for your roll), the strip says so, outlined, with **Continue**, or **Choose…** when it asks for a choice, which opens the trip's panel; if the panel shows something else, a message says so too. When your last step ended on an **important moment** (a pause, a fight, someone gaining a condition, a place made, going into another map, a clock filled, something its system marks as important: the Grey Marches' omen of the Wyrm), the strip tells it in full in that row's place: its title, what happened and what it changed, with **Continue** when the trip waits, else × to put it away ([Important moments up front](../travel/02-playing.md#the-trip)). Going down into another map is one: _You go into The Barrow-halls_. Its ▾ opens the date, the moons and the parts of the day. The panel keeps what you do in sight while the rest scrolls: the buttons, the travel mode (on foot, on horseback…) and, when the main button can't be pressed, why, under it. Drag the panel's left edge to widen it (this browser remembers it). The rest is a tab at a time: **Journal** (the whole trip as moments, newest first, each under its title, the moments of your last step marked: the story, the rules too, or everything that happened behind, such as each roll's dice and why a check didn't come up; see [the journal's filter](../travel/02-playing.md#the-journal-everything-or-the-story)), **Party** (everything about the party in one place: its sheet with the system's party stats, each saying where it comes from, such as _Kael’s Pathfinding, the highest_; its characters, with what they carry; the party token itself keeps nothing, see [The party](../travel/02-playing.md#the-party); the help of a stat, a value or a role says what reads and changes it), **So far** (cells, km, hours marched, checks, actions, the party's values and items spent and gained) and **Options** (the play mode, **New trip**, discovery, the trail, **Skip pauses**, **Stop at every important moment**, **Roll the foes by hand**, **Foes' turns at once**, **Clear trail**, **Remove party**: what's set once a trip, out of the way while playing). See [Playing a trip](../travel/02-playing.md#the-trip).

**Characters**: when the map's system has a sheet, the panel's **Party** tab has a **Characters** section, the same as in Travel ([Characters](../travel/02-playing.md#characters)): add the party's characters, choose who acts, change their values and conditions; the stats the system makes of theirs follow them, and what the party eats is taken from what they carry. They're saved in the map file (as OTD characters of the party), and a map can bring its company before any trip starts: the Grey Marches' example map comes with Kael, Mara and Old Tobin, who start the first trip. A new trip with the same system keeps them. Like Travel's, they can come from the roster and be kept in it ([the roster](../travel/02-playing.md#characters)), and so can the party's own sheet, with its members ([the party in the roster](../travel/02-playing.md#the-party)): the map is the place they travel in. Their relations to the map's places, regions and cells are drawn as thin threads from the party, in their own layer: **Layers → Relations**, hidden until you show it ([Layers](10-layers-and-export.md)).

A trip keeps the system it started with. If its campaign's system changes later (in **Map settings → Campaign**, or by joining another campaign), the panel says so and the trip plays on with its own; **New trip** starts one with the campaign's. Choosing another system in **New trip** makes it the campaign's, asking first if other maps or trips are in it.

**Connections in play**: a connection drawn on the map (a gate, a ford, a hidden path: [Connections](04-roads-and-rivers.md#connections)) that the trip can't cross now, by its kind's **Open when** / **Closed when** in the system and its state in the trip, is drawn greyed with its diamond crossed out; routes go round it, or up to it and stop. What actions and results change of it (a gate opened, a path found) is the trip's, never the map's: selected with the **Connections** tool, its panel says under **In the trip going on** whether it can be crossed now and which of its values the trip changed. The journal says each change.

**Into a sub-map**: on a cell that leads into a [sub-map](13-sub-maps.md), **Go in ↗** in the Play panel (or the cell's) takes it at once; once through, the trip goes on in the sub-map, which opens (the same trip), and the map it left says so in Play. A sub-map with several ways (a tunnel) is crossed: a destination beyond it may be reached through it, in by one way and out by another.

## Checks and the journal

Systems declare their checks (the weather each morning, getting lost, encounters…) and which table resolves each one; the results go to the **journal**, grouped by day. A check without a table is only written in the journal, unless it pauses: then it waits for you to resolve it and press **Continue**. A check or a result can also pause the trip after rolling, and an action can stop it to tell you something or ask you how to go on (the Grey Marches' ferry: [what makes Continue appear](../travel/02-playing.md#the-trip)). Results show what they changed (Food +1, Morale −1…), and actions, what was eaten and fatigue are journaled too, also when nothing happens: see [what the journal says](../travel/02-playing.md#the-trip). With the [world clock](12-world.md#with-a-trip-going-on) running, the trip and the world share one time: moving the clock on is waiting where the party is (dawn's checks, eating, camping at night).

A result may also bring **those present**: characters made from the system's templates where the party is (the Grey Marches' wolves and their leader, each with its own health), kept in the trip until they fall, run or are left behind; the journal says who comes and goes. See [Those present](../travel/02-playing.md#characters). A system whose fights go in turns plays them the same way on the map, with the same fight panel above the Play panel's buttons (everyone as a card with their health and conditions, whose turn it is, the round, the fight's moments, **End the fight**; the panel widens by itself while the fight lasts, and a foe to strike is chosen by clicking its card or its token on the map; each foe's turn waits for **Next** before another foe's, in the panel and the strip) and **Start a fight** while someone is at the party's place, when no action of the system starts one: [A fight in turns](../travel/02-playing.md#characters).

In Play they are drawn on the cells where they were made, from the trip: they are never written into the map's tokens. Up to six tokens on a cell are drawn apart; past that, the copies of one template are one token with how many (**×6**), and what still doesn't fit is a chip (**+3**). Tick **Draw copies apart** in the Play panel to draw every copy on its own, however many. Selecting a cell lists everything on it under **Here**: the party, those present (the same cards as Travel's **Here** box) and the tokens placed by hand.

**Click** one of those present on the map (with the trip's tool or with Select) to select it, outlined as a selected token is, and see its sheet: the trip panel's **Party** tab opens with its card unfolded in **Here** (one left behind elsewhere, under _left behind elsewhere_). **Drag** it to move it in the trip, to another cell or room: a wolf that circles round, a guard that steps aside. The journal says so (_Grey wolf moved to The Old Bridge by hand_); dragged onto the party's place it's among those present again, and put somewhere else it stays there until the party comes, even if its template's copies go once left behind (that's for the party leaving them, not you placing them). Dragging is undone by dragging back, not with Ctrl+Z, and does nothing while the **Tokens** layer is locked.

The bundled **Grey Marches** show all of it, on their example map (**Maps → Example maps**): weather by season, getting lost off roads, encounters by terrain, region, danger and time of day, a toll on the bridge, a ford rolled on an oracle, a boat on the lake, and checks with no table — the trip stops at the standing stones (`landmark`) until you describe the place and press **Continue**. See [The Grey Marches](../bundled/02-grey-marches.md).

Terrain, roads and rivers, the cell's tags, fields and region, the season and today's weather all reach the travel rules and the tables, so a system can make forests slower or roads safe from getting lost. What each system does is in its pack: see [Roads, rivers, walls and borders](04-roads-and-rivers.md#what-roads-and-rivers-do-when-you-travel).

## Moving by hand

**On a trip the party walks.** Drag its token to a cell, or press an **arrow key**: it goes there by the map's ways, through doors and connections and never through a wall, and the system's rules apply as on any march. Where no way leads, it stays put and says so; if the trip can't move on now (lost for the rest of the day, a check waiting for you, a rule of the system), it says why. An arrow (or <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd>) steps the party onto the cell next to it that lies that way, on hexes and squares alike (on hexes, where no cell lies straight that way, whichever of the two nearest that way it can enter); across a door drawn between cells apart, onto the cell beyond it. If a wall stands there, or the system doesn't let the party in (a terrain it can't cross, a door shut), it stays put and says which: it never takes another cell instead, round a corner. In a room (a [region played as one place](03-regions.md#rooms)) each key is one cell: inside the room only where the party stands changes, and no time passes, since to the rules the room is one place (not while the trip waits for you, a check or a fight: it says why); a step onto a door, an opening or a corridor out of it is a step of the trip, with its time and checks, and the party stands on the cell it stepped onto. A piece laid under the party (a map laid piece by piece) takes it in: if it's a room, the party is in the room. Clicked or dragged to a cell of a room, the party goes to the room and stands on that cell, not at the door it came in by; already in the room, it steps across to it at once. In Simple mode, with no rules, dragging puts the party anywhere; to move it by hand on a trip, use **Edit**. Clicking a token (without dragging it) selects it and is a click on its cell too: to travel to the dragon, click the dragon.

## Discovering the map

With a system that can discover (the Grey Marches can; their example map leaves its east blank), tick **Discover the map as you travel**. Start with a blank map: paint only the cell where the party begins and click a destination anywhere. As the party travels, the system's tables decide the **empty** cells:

- **Terrain**, seen from the land you're on (forest tends to go on as forest): by a table, or by a [land model](../technical/07-kinds.md#land-models) that adds up the land all around the cell.
- **What is there**, the first time you enter a cell: a point of interest, tags (a `landmark` stops the Grey Marches' trips there), a name. The trip stops when you find something, so you can play it.
- **Places from templates**: a system with [catalogues of places](../technical/07-kinds.md#places) can make a found place from one (the Grey Marches' ruins, wayside shrines and the dragon's lair): a point of interest with the template's name, text, icon and fields, its tags on the cell (a `ruins` cell can be delved). Any result of a trip can make one on the party's cell the same way (Core's **Explore**, on a 6, a _Landmark_). The point of interest says **Made from** its template, and is yours to edit or remove like any other.

**What is discovered**: _the cells around the party_ (what it sees: their terrain is known before you step in, so routes and speeds are real), _only the cell the party enters_, or, with a system that has [pieces](../packs/24-pieces.md), _through exits, piece by piece_: the map grows a shape at a time where the party goes. The system chooses one; you can change it for your game.

A map that is discovered and has nothing yet says so on opening it in Play, with **Start playing**: the party stands at the way that leads there, or in the middle of the map. A map laid piece by piece starts with its first piece under the party, and an arrow marks each exit nobody has crossed yet.

Cells you painted are never changed, so you can prepare part of the map and leave the rest unknown. Discoveries are part of the game, like the journal: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> doesn't undo them; repaint or delete by hand. Only noteworthy finds go in the journal.

To make your own system discover, see [Connecting tables to maps and trips](../oracle/07-connecting.md#7-discovering-the-map).
