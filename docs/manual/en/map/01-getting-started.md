# Getting started

Map draws maps of hexes or squares for hexcrawls and sandbox campaigns, and lets you play trips on them. Everything stays in your browser: no account, no server. It can be installed and works offline: see [Installing and playing offline](../technical/06-install-and-offline.md).

A browser with no maps yet greets you over an empty map: **New map**, **Import file…**, the examples of the systems you have, or **Start on this empty map**.

## The screen

- **Top bar**, like in every app: on the left the app, the button with nine dots that opens the other OpenTabletop apps, the campaign you're playing, the open map's name (after the maps above it, if it's a sub-map; a click on the name opens Map settings) and the **Edit | Play** switch; on the right Undo and Redo, then New, Maps, Save and Export, then Layers, Map settings, the gear (**Preferences**, shared by every app: language and notes app) and Help (?).
- **Toolbar** (left): in **Edit**, the tools by what they're for — Select; Terrain, Regions, Roads and rivers, Walls; Connections; Icons, Text, Tokens — then the World clock and the Oracle; in **Play**, only Select, The trip, the World clock and the Oracle. Map opens with **Select**, so a first click on the map shows a cell instead of painting it. The small tab on the map's left edge folds it away for more room (the tools' keys still work) and brings it back.
- **Map** (center): drag with the middle button or <kbd>Space</kbd> + drag to pan, use the wheel to zoom, <kbd>F</kbd> fits the whole map.
- **Side panel** (right): what the active tool edits — the selected cell with Select, the palette with Terrain, the selected token with Tokens… — or Settings, Layers, the [Tree of maps](13-sub-maps.md#making-one), the Oracle and the other views of the top bar's buttons. Changing tools deselects what the previous one had selected. The tab on the map's right edge folds it away and brings it back.
- **Under the map**: the cell under the pointer (its coordinate, region, faction and ways to other maps) on the left; on the right the zoom, 100% being the whole map in view, with − and +, and **Fit**.
- **Help column** (far right, opened with **?**): this manual, or the explanation of a field, beside the side panel so you see both.

In a narrow window (under 1100 px) the side panel floats over the map, folded until a tool, a panel or a cell needs it; its tab folds it away. On a phone it's a sheet under the map: **▴** gives it most of the window, and **⋯** in the top bar holds the map's actions.

Every tool has a key: hover a toolbar button to see it, or read [Keyboard shortcuts](11-shortcuts.md).

**Help where you are**: a label underlined with dots has an explanation, often with examples of what to write. Click it and the help column (**?**, on the far right) opens on it, in place of this manual: what the field does, examples that work and, under **In the manual**, the manual's sections about it (the Syntax page first); **← The manual** goes back to the manual. From a field, <kbd>F1</kbd> shows its help. Moving to another field doesn't change what the column shows, so a click on an example there inserts it into the field you're writing in.

**Examples go in with a click**: after you've been in a text box or the YAML editor, click an example in `code` in the help column (in a field's help, on the **Syntax** page or anywhere in the manual) and it goes in where the cursor was; **Ctrl+Z** takes it back. **Syntax**, next to the column's search box, opens the page with everything a pack can write. The search looks in this app's pages, the technical ones and the packs', and shows the words found in bold. Buttons with only an icon say their name when you hover them.

## Your maps

Maps are kept in this browser's library and saved automatically while you work. A map open in two windows (or changed by another app) stays the same in both: what was played elsewhere (a trip, the world clock, factions, the Oracle) just shows up, and Undo keeps working; if the map itself was edited elsewhere, the window shows that version, says so, and Undo starts again. **Maps** (the folder button) lists them by campaign, the one you're playing first (maps alone in a campaign of their own are together, last), each [sub-map](13-sub-maps.md) under the map it hangs from (↳): open one, delete it from this browser or copy its link; **Import file…** opens a saved file. Under **Example maps** are the maps the loaded systems bring (`maps:` in their definition, see [Kinds of definition](../technical/07-kinds.md#systems)), each with the system it's played with: _The Grey Marches_ is a ready map to play and to learn from (see [The Grey Marches](../packs/02-grey-marches.md)).

An example you open becomes **your map**: your changes and your trip are kept in it. Opening the example again, once you've played or changed it, asks whether to **go on with yours**, **open it beside yours** (a fresh copy as another map, yours kept) or **start fresh** in its place (yours is lost, and so is its campaign's world, unless another of your maps or trips is in it). Examples whose files name the same campaign (the Grey Marches' two maps) open in it: the second one joins the campaign the first started, with its world clock and factions. If this browser doesn't have the example's campaign but has others with the same name or played with the same system (a map of that game you started before, or a trip in Travel, which plays in a campaign of its own named after its system), it asks whether to **Join** one of them (the example's own world clock and factions are left out) or **Start its own**. When the only one is the campaign a trip in Travel started by itself (not one you made yourself), the example joins it without asking, and a message says so; to play it apart, choose **Its own** under [**The map is in**](#campaigns) in Map settings. Once one example has joined, the others of its campaign (and their sub-maps) join the same one without asking again. When a newer version of the app brings a changed example, the list marks it **new version** and the question says so: your map keeps playing as it was, and you choose when to try the new one.

**New map** (✚ in the top bar, or in **Maps**) asks what kind of map to start: a **hexcrawl** (hexes, the land's terrains, played at the system's scale) a **dungeon** ([squares](#hexes-or-squares) of 3 m, `cellKm: 0.003`, and the dungeon palette: floors whose walls draw themselves, and rock, see [Terrain](02-terrain.md)) or a **town** (squares of 3 m and the town palette: streets of cobbles, yards, gardens, grass, trees and water outside, and the wooden or stone floors of its buildings inside, drawn in Core's **Village** style, roofs and all: the hybrid of ground outside and structures in). Everything stays changeable in Map settings and **Edit palette**; then it asks whether to save the map you had to a file first.

**Save** writes the map to a file (`.otd.json`, OpenTabletop Data) to back it up or share it; **Maps → Import file…** (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>O</kbd>) opens one again. If the file is a map this browser already has (say, an older backup) and they differ, Map asks whether to **replace** your copy with the file or **keep both** (the file opens as a separate map, with a campaign of its own). A file carries its map's [campaign](#campaigns); if this browser already has that campaign (another map of it, a friend's map of your game) and it differs, Map asks too: **Join it** (the map opens in the campaign kept here, and the file's world clock, factions and Oracle are left out), **Replace mine** (the file's campaign takes the place of the one here, for every map and trip in it) or **Keep apart** (the file's campaign is kept as a new one of its own).

> Clearing the browser's data deletes its library: save files of the maps you care about, or a [backup of everything](../technical/05-backups.md) (app switcher → **Save a backup**), or an [export](../technical/05-backups.md#exporting-and-importing-some-of-it) of the maps and campaigns you choose (app switcher → **Export…**).

## Undo and settings

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> redoes every edit (a whole brush stroke is one step). Playing a trip is not part of undo: it has its own journal.

**Map settings** (the folded map) holds the map name, the grid (its [cells](#hexes-or-squares), coordinates), its size (by number of cells or by paper), the world scale (km per cell, used for travel; empty, the map is played at its system's scale, and you only write one for a map drawn at another) its [campaign](#campaigns), with the system it's played with, and its [style](#styles). Your own preferences (language, the notes app you link to) are under the gear, in **Preferences**: they belong to this browser, not to the map.

## Styles

How a map is drawn is its **style**, chosen in **Map settings → Style**; it never changes what the map holds, its rules or a trip. Styles come from packs, and a system may bring its own. Core brings seven:

- **Schematic**: flat colours and a glyph per terrain, every cell with its coordinates. The look of every map that hasn't chosen another; plain and clear, for planning and printing.
- **Parchment**: a hexcrawl drawn in ink on old paper. Each terrain a light wash of its colour, with its mountains, trees, hills, reeds or waves drawn in ink; borders that wander and blend; the coast inked with ripples along it; roads and rivers that wobble as if drawn by hand; bridges and fords drawn as what they are. The Grey Marches' frontier is drawn in it.
- **Painted**: rich colours melting into each other, mountains and trees lit on one side and shaded on the other, pale shallows along the shores, a paper grain over all; bridges and fords drawn as what they are.
- **Relief**: Painted's ground, with the mountains, hills, forests, dunes and swamps drawn as small pictures (images of a pack, Azgaar's relief icons): a mountain or a hill to a cell, trees in groups.
- **Classic dungeon**: for maps with floor (rooms and corridors): white floor with a faint grid, thick black walls, the rock around them hatched by hand, as old adventure modules draw it; doors and arches in their walls, a way down or up drawn as a stair (a shaft as a ladder). The Barrow-halls are drawn in it.
- **Cave**: rough rock walls that wander off the grid, a pale sandy floor, the rock beyond the walls stippled thick by them and thinning out; the ways out drawn as a cave's mouth (a shaft as a ladder). The old clan mine is drawn in it.
- **Village**: a town or a village seen from above: cobbled streets, yards and gardens in ink over a light wash, trees and water, and each building under its tiled roof, casting its shadow (see **Buildings and roofs** below). Ashford, the Grey Marches' market village, is drawn in it.

The Grey Marches bring one more, the **Engraved atlas**: an ink wash on vellum, the hills, mountains and woods engraved as small pictures, the vellum's texture over the map. The Hollow Hills are drawn in it.

Parchment, Painted and Relief hide the cells' coordinates: the cell's panel and the line under the map still say them, and **Map settings → Show coordinates** shows them again. What is drawn on each terrain comes from its id (the natural palette's terrains all have their art; a terrain of your own has only its colour, unless a style names it). A style may draw a terrain with **images** a pack brings, and lay an image over the whole map as a **texture**: see [Map styles](../systems/20-map-styles.md). Each cell's drawing is its own, and the same every time you open the map.

Choosing a style copies its settings into the map: the names (**Map texts**), the **Regions**, the cells' **Coordinates** and the terrains' glyphs (the Terrain panel), which you then change as you like; Ctrl+Z puts the previous look back. The rest (the paper, the ground, the art, the shores, how roads and rivers are drawn, the grid's line, the floors, the walls and how each kind of connection is drawn) comes from the style while the map is drawn. A map whose style isn't loaded in this browser is drawn in the Schematic one, and Map settings says so. **Buildings and roofs**: in a style with roofs (Village), a **building** (floor cells joined side by side, see [Floor](02-terrain.md)) is seen from outside: a roof over it, a gable over each rectangle of it (an L-shaped house has two), and the shadow it casts. Two houses wall to wall are two buildings when each is its own room ([a region played as one place](03-regions.md#rooms)); the rooms inside a house are one building. Under a closed roof, what the building holds isn't seen: its inner doors, rooms' names, icons, cells' names, note markers and tokens (which can't be clicked either; the party's always shows, since a building it stands in is open). A building **opens**, showing all of it, while the party stands in it, or while one of its cells is selected (click it to draw inside); hide the **Roofs** layer (**Layers**) to open them all while you draw. A style without roofs (every other of Core's) always shows floors and walls, as a dungeon.

**A style of your own**: a bundled style can't change, but **Make a copy** (under **Style**) makes a new style of yours from the map's, in your pack **My map styles**, and draws the map in it. **Edit** then opens its form under the picker, part by part (the paper, the ground, the art of each terrain, shores, roads and rivers, floors, walls, connections, names, regions), and the map shows each change at once; every map drawn in that style follows it. Since names, regions, coordinates and glyphs are copied into the map when the style is chosen, **Take its names and regions again** copies them once more after you change them in the style. The same form is in the Systems app, under [Map styles](../systems/20-map-styles.md), and the YAML of a style in [Kinds of definition](../technical/07-kinds.md#map-styles).

## Hexes or squares

A map's cells are hexes or squares (**Map settings → Cells**). Hexes, six neighbours each, suit the land: overland maps and hexcrawls. **Squares** suit sites: a dungeon, a building, a town's streets, a battle map; each has four neighbours, or eight when the system lets trips step across corners ([Diagonal step](../systems/05-rules.md)). Everything works the same on both: terrain, regions, roads and rivers, icons, tokens, connections, trips (a trip steps from a cell to a neighbour), printing. Hexes also have their **Orientation** (flat or pointy tops). Changing the cells keeps each cell's data under the same column and row, so the drawing shifts a little. A [sub-map](13-sub-maps.md) is made of squares, since it's usually a site inside a place; the Grey Marches' Barrow-halls are one. In this manual, a “cell” is a hex or a square, whichever the map has.

## Campaigns

A map is the **place**; the world it's played in is its **campaign**: the game system, the world clock (the [World](12-world.md) panel), its weather, the factions and the Oracle (decks drawn, once-only results, hand rolls). Every map and every trip of the Travel app is in one campaign, and the maps and trips of the same campaign share all of that: one date, the same factions (each map keeps the land they hold on it), the same decks. A new map starts in a campaign of its own, named after it, so a map played alone works as it always did. Two campaigns never share a name: a new one whose name is taken gets a number (_North (2)_). Opening a map says, for a moment, which campaign it's in and which maps and trips are in it too, and **Playing** in the bar names it from then on, with **Open in Campaign ↗** under it: the campaign you're playing is the open map's, in every app ([The campaign you play in](../campaign/01-getting-started.md#the-campaign-you-play-in)). Map starts on a map of the campaign you're playing (the last one open if it's in it, else its newest). How campaigns come to be, and how to merge two: [Campaign](../campaign/01-getting-started.md#how-campaigns-come-to-be). The [Campaign](../campaign/01-getting-started.md) app shows every campaign this browser keeps, with its world clock, timeline, maps and trips.

**Map settings → Campaign**:

- **The map is in**: **Its own**, or another campaign kept in this browser (a map's, or a Travel trip's). Choosing one moves the map to it. If the map's campaign has a world of its own (a world clock, factions or Oracle rolls), Map asks which one stays: **Keep the campaign's** (the map's world is left behind, and the map keeps the land of the factions the campaign has) or **Bring this one's** (it takes the place of the campaign's world, system included, for every map and trip in it). When the other campaign has no world yet, the choice is to bring it or **Leave it behind**. The campaign the map leaves is deleted if nothing else is in it; under the choice, **Also in it** names the other maps and trips of the campaign.
- **Campaign name**: how the campaign is called wherever it is chosen (here, Travel, the Oracle).
- **System** and **Packs**: the campaign's, so every map and trip in it plays them. Changing the system asks first if other maps or trips are in the campaign; a trip going on keeps the system it started with until a new one ([Play](08-play.md)).

Moving a map to another campaign, renaming the campaign and changing its system are not part of Undo. One trip goes on in a campaign at a time ([With a trip going on](12-world.md#with-a-trip-going-on)).

## Links to cells

Every cell has a link (the chain icon next to its coordinate). Paste it in your notes: opening it shows the map with that cell selected.

The link carries the map's id. If this browser doesn't have the map (another device, or its data was cleared), Map tells you which file it needs — `<map id>.otd.json`, the name **Save** gives it — and offers to open it; once it's loaded you land on the cell.
