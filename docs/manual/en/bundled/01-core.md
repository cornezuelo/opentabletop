# Core

**Core** is the generic pack every app ships with: tools for any game, with no setting, and the **Generic rules**, the smallest travel system, the one a map plays when it chooses none. It's MIT-licensed, written in English with a Spanish translation.

| Definition                                                                                                                                                            | What it's for                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Yes or no?** (oracle)                                                                                                                                               | Ask a yes/no question and choose how likely it is. The classic solo-play oracle.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **How do they take it?** (oracle)                                                                                                                                     | How someone answers a request: the bigger the ask, the harder the yes. Every answer comes with a **Motive**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Prompt** (generator)                                                                                                                                                | An action and a subject (`{{action.text}} {{subject.text}}`) to spark an idea when you're stuck.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Scene twist** (table)                                                                                                                                               | At the start of a scene: on a 1 it doesn't go as expected. Offers advantage when things are calm, disadvantage when tension is high.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Advantage** and **Disadvantage** (roll modes)                                                                                                                       | Roll twice and keep the higher or the lower total. Core's tables offer them, and so can any pack that depends on Core (`modes: [roll-modes.advantage, roll-modes.disadvantage]`).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Against the odds** (table), **Either way** (roll mode) and **A contest** (generator)                                                                                | Roll under a chance in a hundred (`chance`, asked when you roll): at or under it, it happens; doubles (`dice.doubles`: 11, 22… 00) make it more, for better or worse. _Either way_ reads the die with its tens and units swapped when that's lower (`swap`), a 73 as 37. **A contest** (generator): your d% against theirs, the higher wins; with `edge` (asked when you roll) yours alone is rolled with advantage (`modeWhen` on its `roll` field).                                             |
| **Twists** and **Complications**                                                                                                                                      | What changes, and a deck of complications to draw from (a card stays out until you reshuffle).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Playing cards**, **Spanish deck**, **Tarot**, **Lenormand**, **Runes** and **Dominoes** (decks)                                                                     | Six decks from `cards.yaml` to draw by hand: the poker deck of 54 (`suit`, `rank`), the Spanish deck of 40, the tarot of 78 with its meanings, upright or reversed (**Which way up**), Lenormand's 36, the 24 runes of the Elder Futhark and the 28 tiles of a double-six set (`high`, `low`, `pips`).                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Poker hand**, **A card for the scene**, **Three-card reading**, **Celtic cross**, **A character from the tarot**, **Lenormand line** and **Rune cast** (generators) | Hands and readings dealt from those decks (`deck: deck.tarot`), the first card with `shuffle: true`, so each one starts from the whole deck and never repeats a card. **A card for the scene** reads what its suit speaks of (`when: { card.suit: spades }`).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **A faction's turn** (table)                                                                                                                                          | What a faction does on a world turn, for any game: expands (+1 cell), holds, schemes or loses ground. A system's factions roll it with `turn: core/table.faction-turn`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **A growing threat** (clocks)                                                                                                                                         | A progress clock for any game: the threat grows a segment at a time and, when full, starts over. A system brings it with `clocks: core/clocks.threat`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **The threat strikes** (world rules)                                                                                                                                  | The world's reaction to it, the smallest one: when the threat fills (`on: world.clocks.the-threat-grows.full`), a line on the timeline and one of the **Twists**, then the world waits for you to think about it (`pause:`: **Go on**). A system brings it with `world: core/world.threat`.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Schematic** (map style)                                                                                                                                             | How a map is drawn when it chooses no other style ([Styles](../map/01-getting-started.md#styles)): flat colours and a glyph per terrain, every cell with its coordinates, names in IM Fell with a light halo, regions with a light tint and an inner border. The smallest [style](../technical/07-kinds.md#map-styles), in `styles.yaml`.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Parchment** and **Painted** (map styles)                                                                                                                            | Two [styles](../map/01-getting-started.md#styles) with art for every terrain of the natural palette: ink on old paper (washed colours, painters in ink, rippled shores, roads and rivers that wobble) and a painted map (blended colours, art lit and shaded, shallows). They show every key of a style: `paper`, `ground`, `art` (a painter per terrain id, `ink` and `painted`), `shores`, `paths`, and `connections`: bridges and fords drawn as what they are.                                |
| **Relief** (map style)                                                                                                                                                | Painted's ground with the land's relief as pictures: mountains, hills, forests, dunes, swamps and volcanoes drawn by images of another pack, **Azgaar's relief icons** ([its page](04-azgaar-relief.md); Core depends on it), a mountain or a hill to a cell, trees in groups; water and grass keep their painters. It shows art by images (`art.terrains.<terrain>.images`, `count`).                                                                                                            |
| **Classic dungeon** and **Cave** (map styles)                                                                                                                         | Two [styles](../map/01-getting-started.md#styles) for maps with floor, made for the dungeon palette (rubble and pools have art): white floor with a faint grid, black walls and the rock hatched; and rough walls on ragged ground with the rock stippled. They show a style's `floors` (`color`, `wash`, `grid`), `walls` (`outside`: `hatch`, `stipple`; `band`) and `connections` by kind: doors, arches and gates in their walls, the ways between maps as stairs, ladders or a cave's mouth. |
| **Village** (map style)                                                                                                                                               | A [style](../map/01-getting-started.md#styles) for towns, made for the Town palette: cobbles (their own painter), yards and gardens in ink, and buildings under tiled roofs that open while the party is in them. It shows a style's `roofs` (`kind`, `color`, `shadow`).                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

Every one is **tagged** with what it's about (`scenes`, `inspiration`, `questions`, `people`, `cards`; _A faction's turn_ also `factions` and `rules`, since a system's world turn rolls it), so the Oracle's **Filter** finds them: `−rules` leaves what you'd roll by hand.

## The Generic rules

Core's system (`system.yaml`, `id: default`, so its id is `core`) is the smallest one that plays: a map whose campaign chooses no system plays it (**Map settings → Campaign → System**), and so do maps and trips of older versions that played the rules built into the apps. Like any bundled system, the Packs app shows it read-only; **New system** starts from a copy of its rules, to change as you like. For one that uses everything, see [The Grey Marches](02-grey-marches.md).

| Part                                                      | What it does, and which feature it shows                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Generic rules** (system)                                | Names its parts: `travel: travel-rules.default`, `bindings: bindings.default`, `calendar: calendar.default`, `weather: [weather.default]`, `land: [land.default]`, `sheet: sheet.character`, `partySheet: sheet.party`, `mapSheet: sheet.map`, `items: [items.gear]`, `bestiary: [bestiary.foes]`. A system that names no `travel` doesn't travel; this one does.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Travel rules** (`travel.yaml`)                          | Played at 10 km a cell (`travel: { cellKm: 10 }`), on foot (30 km a day) or on horseback (50 km), across the terrains of Map's palettes (a dungeon's floors walked, rubble at half speed, pools and solid rock closed), faster on roads and trails, and through a **gate** drawn on a map (Map's **Connections**: ten minutes more, `extra: 10`; shut until its state says open, `passable: { when: { connection.state: open } }`, which **Open the gate** changes for the trip, `effects: { connection.state: open }`, offered `when: { all: [{ connection.kind: gate }, { not: { connection.state: open } }] }`), and a **way in** to a [sub-map](../map/13-sub-maps.md) (`way-in`, five minutes more, `extra: 5`); a river drawn on a map is waded where no road or bridge crosses it, an hour more (`river: { crossing: { extra: 60 } }`, read as `trip.crossing: river`); water isn't crossed, and a lake only on the ice in winter (`passable: { when: { time.season: winter } }`). Its day is named by its calendar's parts: trips start as the morning begins (`start: time.part.morning`), and a party that can't march camps from the night (`halt: { at: time.part.night, do: actions.camp }`). Food is a value of the party's sheet (below), eaten a day at a time as each day ends. One check, **Weather** (`WEATHER_CHECK_REQUIRED`), as each morning begins (`at: time.part.morning`), which the bindings roll on _The plain sky_ (`weather: weather.default`); rain makes the march ×0.75, a storm or snow ×0.5 (`weather: { rain: { speed: 0.75 } … }`). |
| **March**, **Camp**, **Rest**, **Eat**, **Explore**       | Five actions made of steps. **March** walks while it isn't dark for the day's 8 hours (`when: { time.tags: { not: dark }, trip.marched: { lt: '{{system.hoursPerDay}}' } }`), to the destination or one cell (a parameter, `travel.move: '{{params.until}}'`), and the trip takes it when it goes on by itself (`travel.onward`). **Camp** sleeps until the morning (`time: time.part.morning`); **Rest** is an hour, only by day; **Eat** is taken by the system itself as each day ends (`on: time.day-end`), using 1 food of the party's sheet (`party.values.food: -1`), and when there was none left the journal says “There is nothing left to eat.” (a `say` step with its own condition, `when: { trip.below: party.values.food }`: a later step sees what the one before it changed). **Explore** is an hour by day, a line saying what comes next (`say`), then an idea of what the party finds rolled on Core's **Prompt** (`roll: generator.prompt`: a step that rolls something of the Oracle, its result in the journal by its name), and then _A place found?_ (`roll: table.place-found`), which on a 6 makes a **Landmark** on the party's cell. The Generic rules have no progress clocks, so no moments of the world (`world.clocks.<id>.full`): the Grey Marches show them. The steps that wait for the player (`pause`, `ask`), change the way of travelling or wait for something are left to systems that need them (the Grey Marches' ferry): see [Actions and steps](../technical/10-actions-and-steps.md).                                      |
| **Despair**                                               | The smallest moment written as a condition (a reaction): as the party's morale reaches 0 (`on: { when: { party.values.morale: { lte: 0 } } }`), whatever lowered it, the trip stops (`pause:`) for you to decide; once, until morale rises and falls to 0 again.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Fight** and **Strike**                                  | The smallest [fight in turns](../technical/07-kinds.md#systems) (`encounter:` in its system: the party first, `actions: [actions.strike]`, over when `present.count: 0`). **Fight**, with foes here, starts it (`effects: { encounter: start }`) and says how tough the toughest of them is (`{{present.highest.values.health}}`); on each character's turn **Strike** asks whom and rolls _A strike_: 1d3 health from that foe (`characters[params.foe].values.health`), and a glancing 1 staggers it for a round (`characters[params.foe].conditions.staggered: 1`, the sheet's _Staggered_ with `lasts: rounds`), so it loses its turn (the system's `skip: { acting.conditions: staggered }`, read as each turn begins) and is steady again as the next round begins. On each foe's turn its table _A foe's turn_ (`foes: table.foe-turn`) strikes one of you, rolled (`set: { target: party.members.random }`), for 1d3 (`characters[target].values.health`), or on a 6 sweeps at all of you (`set: { target: party.members.all }`), each losing their own 1d2.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Mend**                                                  | The smallest action for each member (`on: actions.rest.done`, `for: party.members`): after a rest, each one still standing (`when: { acting.values.health: { gte: 1 } }`) gets 1 health back (`acting.values.health: 1`), one after another, each acting. See [Your own travel system](../oracle/07-connecting.md).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Time** (a group of buttons)                             | The smallest group of buttons (`groups: { time: … }`): Camp and Rest under _Time_ (`group: groups.time`), the march first and the rest after it, as without groups. See [Groups of buttons](../packs/05-rules.md#groups-of-buttons).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Search the fallen**                                     | The smallest action on one of those present leaving (`on: present.leave`, the one leaving acting): a foe that fell (`when: { acting.conditions: down }`) rolls _What the fallen carry_, a day's food on a 5 or 6 (`party.values.food: 1`). See [Bestiaries](../technical/07-kinds.md#bestiaries).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **The plain sky** (`weather.yaml`)                        | The smallest weather model for any setting: clear, cloudy, rain, storm and, in winter only, snow, today's following yesterday's by chance tables per season (`next`), so rain lasts a few days and storms blow over. A kind a season leaves out (snow in spring) starts over from the season's `start`. Shows [weather with inertia](../packs/13-weather.md); the Grey Marches show a hex flower too.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **The plain land** (`land.yaml`)                          | The smallest land model: plains, farmland and forests, hills rising into mountains, lakes and their marshes, each tending to go on as itself (`next`), mostly open land where nothing is known (`start`). Map's **Generate** fills empty cells with it; discovery stays off. Shows [land models](../technical/07-kinds.md#land-models).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **The caves** (`land.yaml`)                               | The same under the ground: rock with caves in it, rubble and pools inside. On squares, walls follow the floor by themselves.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **The plain calendar** (`calendar.yaml`)                  | Four seasons of 90 days, one month each; a day in four parts (morning from 06:00, afternoon, evening, night from 20:00, tagged `dark`: `time.part`, `time.tags`) and six watches of four hours (`time.watch`, 1 to 6; the World panel's **Next: …**).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Character** and **Party** (sheets)                      | The smallest sheets: a character's **Health** (3, never below 0 nor above 3, drawn as a bar) and **Wounded**, for you to track by hand, with the two parts that hold by themselves at their smallest: **Harm**, made of Health (`from: '{{3 - self.values.health}}'`), and **Down**, while Health is 0 (`when: { self.values.health: { lte: 0 } }`); the party's own **Morale** (3, from 0 to 5), drawn as boxes (`display: boxes`), and its **Food** (6 for a new trip, never below 0): what the party carries, as a plain value of its sheet, the smallest way. The smallest characters for the roster too: **New character** in the [Characters app](../characters/01-getting-started.md) with the Generic rules makes one, and a trip with them takes it with **From the roster**; a **Party** made there (**Sheet**: _a party_) is the smallest party to carry from trip to trip, its Food left as it was.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **This map** (sheet)                                      | The smallest [sheet of each map](../technical/07-kinds.md#sheets) (`mapSheet: sheet.map`): a value, **Explored**, which **Explore** raises (`map.values.explored: 1`), and the smallest die that steps, the **Finds die** (`steps: [4, 6]`, `display: die`): _A place found?_ rolls it (`'1d{{max(map.values.finds, 4)}}'`), and a landmark found drops it from a d6 to a d4 (`map.values.finds: -1`), so a map has one. Each map keeps its own, and a trip finds it again when it comes back.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Gear** (`items.yaml`)                                   | The smallest item catalogue: a rope, a torch, a ration and a shield, with tags (`climbing`, `light`, `food`, `armour`), in no place of the sheet, so they're a plain list with counts, and one field, the shield's **Wear**: a die that steps (`wear: { steps: [4, 6], display: die }`), a d6 that drops to a d4 when a foe's blow lands on whoever carries it (_A foe's turn_: `characters[target].items.shield.wear: -1`, nothing for whoever carries none); ten things in all (the sheet's `items.room: 10` with `over: refuse`: what's given past it is left behind, said in the journal). **Add an item** on a character's card offers them; effects can give them (`acting.items.torch: 1`). One **ability**, _Tough_ (tagged `ability`), goes only to the sheet's place _Abilities_ (`takes: { item.tags: ability }`, `carried: false`, shown as cards): it weighs nothing, and the sheet's `tough` (`from: { count: items, where: { item.id: tough } }`) raises the most health to 4. Shows [items](../packs/08-items.md); the Grey Marches show fields, places and containers.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Foes** and **Trouble on the road** (`bestiary.yaml`)    | The smallest [bestiary](../technical/07-kinds.md#bestiaries): one template, **Foe**, on the characters' own sheet, its Health rolled for each copy (`'{{1d3}}'`); and a table that makes them, as many as its `set` rolls and its text says (`present.foe: '{{foes}}'`), on a die that explodes once (`1d3!1`: on a 3, another 1d3 come). Rolled by hand during a trip, the foes are among those present (`present.foe.count`); one whose Health reaches 0 is **Down**, and gone (the sheet's _Down_ `leaves`).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Places** and **A place found?** (`places.yaml`)         | The smallest [catalogue of places](../technical/07-kinds.md#places): one template, **Landmark**, with a text, an icon of the map's (`game:obelisk`) the tag it gives its cell (`landmark`) and a value the rules read there (`shelter: 1`: **Camp** says so, `place.poi.shelter`); and a table that makes it (`effects: { place.pois.landmark: 1 }`), rolled by **Explore**. On a map in the Map app it becomes a point of interest **Made from** _Landmark_; in a trip without a map, a line in the journal.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Rooms and passages** and **Next piece** (`pieces.yaml`) | The smallest [pieces](../packs/24-pieces.md): a passage, a junction, a room, stairs down and a dead end, the same five on squares and on hexes with the same ids, one table for both, `pieces.total` for the stairs and the dead end; the stairs are an important moment, shown up front (`notable: true`); the stairs' far exit leads into a new map (`newMap: true`), a level below named after the map. A map on the Generic rules with **Discover the map as you travel** on grows through its doors                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

**In a campaign.** A [campaign](../campaign/01-getting-started.md) that plays the Generic rules (a new one in the Campaign app, or any map whose campaign chooses no system) gets a world clock in the plain calendar: its date in seasons, **Next: …** by its parts of the day and its watches, its weather rolled day by day on _The plain sky_ (a trip's, while one goes on), events and progress clocks you add by hand. The Generic rules declare no factions and no clocks of their own, since powers and threats belong to a setting: a system of yours brings Core's **A growing threat** (`clocks: core/clocks.threat`, and what it does when full with `world: core/world.threat`) and gives its factions **A faction's turn** (`turn: core/table.faction-turn`). For a campaign with all of it (two maps, factions, clocks, events), see [The Grey Marches](02-grey-marches.md).

Other packs depend on Core (the Grey Marches use its tables), so it isn't copied in place: **Duplicate as a new pack** on its page in the Packs app makes a pack of yours from it (`core-mine`), to change as you like, while the bundled Core stays for the packs that need it and keeps its updates. A copy of Core made by an older version still replaces it; the Packs app says when a system uses one older than the bundled Core and lets you take its changes.
